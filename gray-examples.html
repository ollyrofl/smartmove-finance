How `#464B4C` could elevate the brand.

This gray works best as a premium balancing color, not as a replacement for your teal. I’d use it to make the site feel more grounded, more polished, and a little less one-note, while keeping teal as the main signature.

How I’d Use It

1. Stronger Secondary Text

Use the gray for body copy, subtitles, and longer descriptive text. That keeps teal for headings and CTAs, and immediately makes the site feel more balanced.

  • Paragraph text
  • Form helper text
  • Footer copy and legal text

2. Premium Dark Surfaces

The gray works beautifully for testimonial cards, stat panels, dropdown backgrounds, and footer areas where pure teal can feel too bright.

  • Dark card backgrounds
  • Secondary CTA buttons
  • Hero or content overlays

3. Cleaner Neutrals

Create a pale gray family from it for borders, chips, and soft backgrounds. That gives the site a more cohesive neutral system than relying mostly on teal tints.

  • Dividers and outlines
  • Input fields and cards
  • Muted label treatments
